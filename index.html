Founder & President
KAS Cosplay Club
Kaohsiung American School · The first cosplay club on campus

I founded KAS Cosplay Club because cosplay had given me so much happiness — a space to create freely, grow more confident, and connect with people who loved what I loved. I wanted to share that feeling, not keep it to myself. What began as a club for students to explore cosplay grew into something more meaningful: bringing characters to life for children at the Love and Hope Child Care Center (愛與希望兒少關懷中心) through costumes, games, dance, and photography. Watching something that made me happy become a source of happiness for someone else changed what cosplay meant to me — the club became not just a creative community, but a way to turn something I love into something I could give.

Materia

A free material companion for junior and self-taught artists. Look up any paint, resin, ink, or glue to see how it behaves and what it safely mixes with. Get it right the first time instead of wasting supplies.

Why I Made It

Like a lot of self-taught artists, I learned what works by ruining things: a resin coat that never cured, paint that lifted the second I layered over it, an afternoon of allowance gone. The knowledge is out there, but it's buried in forum threads and product pages you only read after something's already gone wrong.

Younger artists run into this constantly. They want to experiment, but every wrong guess costs materials, time, and confidence. Materia is the tool I wish I'd had: one place that tells you how a material behaves and what it works with, before you commit.

Pain point 01
Buying on a guess

I'd pick materials off a vibe and a thumbnail, then find out mid-piece they didn't work together.

Pain point 02
Wasted money

One wrong combo could ruin a whole piece and the supplies with it. On a student budget, that's real money gone.

Pain point 03
Answers scattered everywhere

The info existed, but across ten forum tabs, PDFs, and buried comments. Never in one place when I needed it.

Pain point 04
Finding out too late

I'd only learn a material's quirk after it cracked, yellowed, or peeled. Too late to undo.

The Process
Phase 01
Idea Selection
  • Listed 20 pain points; ran 5 user interviews
  • Scored the top 10 with ICE, then Fast SWOT on each
  • Wrote a Lean Canvas for the top 3 and interviewed people on them
  • Converged on the material-compatibility problem
Phase 02
Build
  • Defined MVP scope; studied 5+ competitors
  • Drew the v1 user flow and low-fidelity screens
  • Named it Materia, built the logotype and brand
  • Shipped a working prototype + landing page with waitlist
Phase 03
Distribution
  • Designed a demo storyline: problem → solution → outcome
  • Mapped distribution channels and a feedback loop
  • Set a one-line positioning and next steps
  • Now putting Materia in front of real first users

Fashion

States of Preservation

Fashion Collection  ·  Process Book  ·  Fantasy → Reality

Theme & Concept. This collection explores death not as a single ending but as a state of perception. It moves from fantasy to reality, so that the viewer first falls for the beauty of a preserved specimen and only afterward recognises what it represents, mirroring exactly what happens when someone sees a diaphonized organism: captivated before they realise they are looking at something dead.

Fantasy. At first glance, the collection presents death as something delicate, vibrant, and almost dreamlike. It is inspired by diaphonized specimens, preserved organisms whose transparent tissues and vividly stained skeletons create an illusion of vitality despite no longer being alive. Bright colours, translucent materials, and flowing silhouettes transform anatomical structures into elegant, wearable forms. Here death appears not as something frightening but as something preserved and beautiful, inviting viewers to question whether death must always be tied to darkness and decay.

Reality. As the collection progresses, the fantasy gradually dissolves into reality. The vibrant transparency gives way to muted, earthy tones, and smooth, delicate surfaces become rough, frayed, and fragmented, echoing the natural process of decomposition. The preserved beauty of the specimen is stripped away to reveal the physical reality of death, where flesh decays, colour fades, and the body slowly returns to nature. By presenting fantasy before reality, the collection asks how perception shapes our emotional response. The organism itself has not changed; only the way it is presented has. It leaves a single question: if I found it beautiful before I knew it represented death, what does that say about how I see death?

About

Chloe

Artist  ·  Designer  ·  Cosplayer  ·  Taiwan, Kaohsiung

Hi, I'm Chloe, a high school student and visual artist based in Kaohsiung, working across cosplay, traditional art, and design. I'm drawn to highly saturated color to express intensity: overwhelm, tension, and sensory overload. Much of my work explores the complex inner emotions of adolescent girls, and how color can communicate a psychological state rather than just an aesthetic.

I'm just as interested in where fashion and art meet. Through cosplay I explore how clothing, makeup, and presentation carry cultural meaning and reference history, taking something often seen as surface-level and revealing the thought behind it. That idea drives my personal magazine, FLAIR, where I analyze character designs and connect them to fashion history and visual storytelling.

I founded the first cosplay club at my school, directed themed shoots with photographers, and produced two volumes of a collaborative magazine. I also led outreach with the Love and Hope Child Care Center, working with over 30 children through performances and creative activities. I've attended 35+ conventions, and many of my ideas begin with noticing a small detail and translating it into something visual.
